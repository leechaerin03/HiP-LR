Motivation

Abstract

LLM-based planners produce symbolic plans that look correct on paper but break down the moment they meet execution. Actions take an unknown amount of time, skills fail, and robots contend for the same physical space. We refer to this mismatch as the grounding gap.

HiP-LR closes this gap by feeding scene-conditioned predictions of action duration and failure probability directly into a closed-loop scheduler, so that coordination decisions are made before contention forms rather than repaired after the fact. When a failure does occur, recovery is localized: it is contained at the skill level instead of triggering a replan of the whole task hierarchy. We evaluate HiP-LR on long-horizon household rearrangement with heterogeneous robot teams in AI2-THOR.

Method

HiP-LR consists of four modules that connect skill-level grounding to team-level scheduling.

M1

PTD modeling

Phase-type distributions with dual absorbing states model how long a skill takes and whether it succeeds, conditioned on the scene.

M2

Failure detection

Skill-level failures are caught at the execution layer, where the evidence actually is.

M3

Shared memory & recovery

Recovery stays local, using team-shared execution context instead of restarting the symbolic plan.

M4

Allocation & scheduling

Failure probability enters as a cost term, and the scheduler assigns explicit start times, not merely an ordering.
